Week 9

Digital Technologies in the Classroom

Finding Digital Resources: Years 7–10

Use this decision tree to find a digital resource for your classroom. Start in the middle, follow the branches, and click a bubble to open or close it.

Recommendation 1 – Year 9, Environmental Sustainability, Understanding Sea Level Change (sealevel.nasa.gov)

Syllabus addressed: SC5-ENV-01 - Investigate and report on the consequences of climate change
The NASA Earth data website Sea Level Change provides a great resource through Falloon’s (2020) TDC framework. It consists of authoritative, real data sets that students can explore to investigate the effect of climate change on the rise in Earth’s sea levels, combining the how and why of digital technologies with the curriculum content. On the personal-ethical competency level, it allows users to browse with low personal data risk and, as an authoritative website by NASA, provides discussion opportunities with students about data security and reliable information online. On the level of people, society and environment, this website offers a strong bridge to Falloon’s ethical dimension. Through teaching students about rising sea levels and climate change, the teacher can deliberately connect these issues to physical technology, such as the short lifetime of hardware and the exploration of resources needed to make these. A pedagogical caveat is the potential overwhelm students can feel through the dense amount of data. A strong scaffold provided by the teacher might be needed as support. .

Recommendation 2 – Year 8, Periodic Table and Atomic Structure, PhET Interactive Build an Atom (phet.colorado.edu)

Syllabus addressed: SC4-PRT-01 - Describe the location, relative charge and mass of protons, neutrons and electrons using the planetary atomic model
This PhET interactive has strong TPACK qualities and thus addresses the curriculum dimension of the TDC framework well (Falloon, 2020). The interactive helps the teacher to guide the students through the how (interacting with the atomic models) and the why (understanding the abstract concept of subatomic particles). On the personal-professional competency level, PhET provides a large global community where educators can share ideas and resources to use alongside the interactive, aligning with the professional network and continuous learning dimension of the TDC framework. On a differentiation level, this interactive can be used in a highly scaffolded way to support learners in working towards achievement levels, as well as a more open-ended inquiry approach for students working ahead. While the simplified visualisation of the model helps conceptual understanding, teachers must be careful around misconceptions that could arise. Quantum mechanical reality looks very different, which students might not be aware of.

Recommendation 3 - All Years, ChatGPT as Personal Tutor for Studying (chatgpt.com)

From a TPACK perspective, ChatGPT can provide instant quizzes, personalised revision questions or explain complex concepts in simpler terms. Teachers need to guide students on how to prompt effectively to gain insights into why a problem was solved. The most critical dimension of this digital resource is personal and ethical competency. Data privacy, biased information and potential AI hallucinations need to be addressed carefully (Furze, 2025). Teachers need to explicitly teach their students these digital literacy skills to use this AI platform safely. Overall, it provides an interdisciplinary approach to digital technology use, linking writing, ethics, subject content and environmental issues, which Falloon (2020) advocates.
