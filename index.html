Welcome.

Image: My classroom displaying a digital-tech-free periodic table.

To my Portfolio

My name is Anne and this is my portfolio for the subject Classroom Technologies in Session 602026. Considering how much technology surrounds us, and hence the classroom as well, I am very much looking forward to this subject and to learning about it in the next few weeks. Since I started working as a teacher about 2.5 years ago, the classroom has changed significantly in how technology is used due to AI. My students are very dependent on it, which puzzles me. It seems like the muscle memory for Google Lens has developed so quickly that they sometimes don’t even try to think about the answers themselves anymore. That is concerning to me. I am sure there are solutions and I am excited to learn. Further, my school introduced tech-free Tuesdays about a year ago, during which students are not allowed to use screens, and will implement a new 50% tech-free rule in every lesson this coming term. The research this decision is based on I haven’t seen yet, though since starting this subject I am quite curious.
On the other hand, technology is extremely productive. I use Google Classroom every day; it is a great way to file lesson content and communicate with students. Further, in Science especially, the world of interactives is amazing and students can grasp abstract concepts through technology like never before (Dawson et al., 2019). While teaching, I can draw and write on my iPad, and students can see it on the TV. I use random name generators, Kahoot quizzes and speech-to-text as differentiation for students, just naming a few of the opportunities to design multiple means in the classroom (CAST, 2025; McGhie-Richmond & de Bruin, 2015). Google Forms provide an excellent way for formative assessment and instant feedback, both of which are crucial in supporting students’ learning (Department of Education and Training, 2020). School management systems have data at your fingertips to plan for and evaluate teaching and learning (Fisk, 2023). Further, the internet provides teacher-approved worksheets on Teacher Pay Teachers and social media teacher groups are an excellent way to professionally engage with colleagues (Australian Institute for Teaching and Leadership [AITSL], 2022; IXL Learning, 2026).
